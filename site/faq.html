Frequently asked questions

How can speed diverge while energy stays finite?

Energy depends on speed squared integrated over volume. In the paper, the region of intense motion shrinks quickly enough for its energy to decrease even as its speed grows. Near the center, fluid spirals inward and exits along the axis; the core becomes progressively thinner relative to its height.

The hard part is keeping the applied force smooth all the way through the singular time. The paper uses oscillatory pulses and successive corrections to cancel otherwise singular terms in the momentum balance. Our finite pulse families and numerical forcing do not reproduce or certify that entire construction. Tracking the target is a solver check; proving those cancellations is a separate mathematical task.

Do we save the full 192³ field?

Yes. The current run saves all three components of velocity and force in every one of its 7,077,888 cells at each scheduled field-output time. The dense archive uses float32; the solver and restart checkpoints stay in float64. We run one full-resolution production simulation at a time and keep its archive on the simulation machine.

The 2D movies extract native 192² planes directly from those saved fields. The interactive 3D explorer uses a lighter 32³ copy, made after capture, so it can play in a browser. Fine structures may be absent from that browser copy, but they remain available in the full archive for later visualization. The mesh diagram shows the actual 192³ solver grid.

This run restarted from rest with native capture enabled. The earlier run saved only 32³ samples at frequent output times; those old files have been preserved separately and are not mixed into the new history. Saving more cells does not establish spatial convergence.

How much storage does that take?

Each full velocity-and-force snapshot holds six float32 values per cell: about 170 MB. The planned 487 field snapshots total about 82.7 GB before compression. Another 12 sparse float64 snapshots require about 4.1 GB before compression, plus restart and temporary files. We budget a 20 GiB disk reserve. Float32 saves half the space of float64 and rounds the stored values to roughly seven significant digits; it does not reduce the grid resolution or the precision used to advance the solver.

Why do the movies hold frames and slow down?

Saved states are held for different durations to follow the gaps in simulation time. GIFs use explicit frame durations; MP4s repeat frames. The last few active intervals enter labeled slow motion so you can inspect the flow, followed by a short final hold. The timestamp always identifies the actual saved state. No new fluid states are interpolated.

The current exporter gives the main segment and the slow-motion segment up to four seconds each, with a half-second final hold. Timing is quantized to 20 milliseconds for matching GIF and video playback. The interactive 3D controls step through saved checkpoints independently; their play button is a browsing aid, not a simulation-time clock. Published frame times and playback durations

How much computation does the run require?

The homepage shows a rough 100–400 TFLOP planning budget for the current 192³ run from rest through t = 0.985. A TFLOP is a trillion floating-point operations; FLOP/s would be a processing rate. This estimate describes the solver run, not work performed by your browser.

The configured output clocks, maximum timestep, diffusion limit, and forcing-phase limit give about 4,113 active steps. Each RK2 step makes two pressure projections, each requiring three forward and three inverse complex 3D Fourier transforms. Using the conventional approximation 5 N log₂(N) per transform, with N = 192³, gives about 40 TFLOP for those transforms. This is an operation-count convention, not a hardware measurement. FFT benchmark methodology ↗

We allow 3–10 times that FFT work for the full calculation, including repeated target and force evaluations and finite-difference kernels. That multiplier is an explicit planning assumption, not a profile or a rigorous bound. Velocity-dependent CFL limits may require more steps. Restarts, validation runs, and rendering are excluded. How is peak speed measured?

Peak speed is the largest velocity magnitude across the full solver grid at the latest saved diagnostic time. That clock can lag the movie frames. It is reported in model units, with its own timestamp.
